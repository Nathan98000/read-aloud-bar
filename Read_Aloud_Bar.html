Read Aloud Bar.

A floating speech controller for any page. Sentence-level navigation and highlighting, article-only extraction, and settings that persist.

Try it first

This is the real thing, running here

Press play. The current sentence highlights as it reads; the skip buttons move exactly one sentence.

The trouble with reading at speed is that the eye is a poor pacemaker. It slows for a difficult clause, then races through a familiar one. You finish a long essay with no clear sense of where the time went. Speech has no such problem. A voice set to three hundred words a minute holds that pace whether the sentence is easy or hard. This is uncomfortable for about a week. Then it becomes the only way you want to get through a backlog.

What the bar does

    
    
      
  • Highlight tints the sentence being spoken. It paints through the CSS Custom Highlight API where available and floating overlay rectangles otherwise — neither one edits the page's markup. The tint stays put while paused and clears on stop.
    • 
      
  • Scroll keeps the current sentence in view, nudging the page only when the sentence drifts near an edge. Turn it off to read along at your own pace, or to keep your place while the voice runs ahead. It works independently of highlighting — either can be on without the other.
    • 
      
  • ◀◀ ▶▶ move exactly one sentence, whatever the internal chunking is doing. While playing, narration continues from the new sentence; while paused, the position moves and playback stays paused.
    • 
      
  • The counter reads 14 / 87 sentences for whatever you're reading — a selection if you made one, the article if you didn't.
    • 
      
  • On Substack, the byline, date, like and comment and restack counts, and the share controls are excluded before extraction; title, subtitle, headings, lists, quotes and captions are kept. Elsewhere the same idea applies to nav, footers, cookie notices, signup prompts, comment threads and recommendation widgets.
    • 
      
  • Voice, speed, and the highlight and scroll settings persist in localStorage across pages. Ava Online is the default on a fresh install, Samantha next, then whatever's available — but a saved choice always wins.
    • 
      
  • Punctuation reaches the engine untouched, so sentences keep their normal falling intonation and end-of-sentence pause.
